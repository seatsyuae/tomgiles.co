In his own words: nine marathons in nine days for Jenson

Tom Giles on running from Bath to Land's End for his son Jenson and CureSHANK, told in his own words from podcasts, interviews and his own posts.

Tom GilesFounder and Managing Director, Seatsy · 14 November 2026

In the summer of 2025, Tom Giles ran from Bath to Land's End: nine marathons in nine consecutive days, from 27 June to 5 July, for CureSHANK, the charity funding research into Phelan McDermid Syndrome. His son Jenson lives with the condition.

Tom, the cofounder and Managing Director of Seatsy, based between Dubai and the UK, has talked about the run on podcasts, in the press and on his own channels. This is the story in his words, with a link to every source.

Why take it on

When he announced the challenge in February 2025, Tom was honest about his starting point.

“I have only run one marathon prior to this, so this will test me in every possible way”

Tom on LinkedIn, February 2025

On the Ambitious Minds podcast with Jay Lawrence, he explained why he chose something so big.

“recently took on a challenge where I ran nine marathons in nine days. I did that because I wanted to capture attention and once you've got attention people will listen.”

Tom on the Ambitious Minds podcast, from 41:45

Who he was running for

Jenson collapsed when he was two days old and spent the first weeks of his life on life support at Bristol Children's Hospital.

“there was a point in time where I didn't think that he would survive. So, he was on life support for weeks in Bristol.”

Tom on the Ambitious Minds podcast, from 41:00

He was later diagnosed with Phelan McDermid Syndrome and Ring 22, a combination so rare that only a few hundred people in the world share it. Speaking to the BBC, Tom described the boy behind the diagnosis as “funny, handsome and obsessed with cars, tractors and bikes”, adding: “He has a smile that would light up a room”.

“Jenson is the most loving and handsome boy. He lights up every room.”

Tom speaking to GB News

Bath to Land's End

The route ran through Glastonbury, Chard, Exeter, Okehampton, Liskeard, Charlestown, Falmouth and Penzance before the finish at Land's End. Most days came in well over marathon distance, something Tom was quick to point out when his cofounder Lewis Allsopp brought the run up on The Seatsy Story: “You're doing me a disservice there as well. It was pretty much ultramarathons.”

“This was the hardest thing I’ve ever done. The adrenaline on the first few days was insane, but it wasn’t until day four that I truly struggled.”

Tom speaking to GB News

Family joined him along the way. His daughter ran the closing miles of some stages with him, and Jenson was there with him at the finish line at Land's End.

“She finished a few of the runs with me, and we’ve got videos of our two-year-old crossing the finish line with me. It was very emotional.”

Tom speaking to GB News

Writing on LinkedIn afterwards, he summed up the distance as “392 kilometres and 509,640 steps away from the start line in Bath on 27th June.”

What it started

The run raised more than £24,000 for CureSHANK and sparked Race for Research, a global campaign with runners across five continents. As Tom told Somerset Live: “What started as 'Run for Jenson' turned into 'Race for Research'”.

“Every step I take is for Jenson and the thousands of people worldwide living with PMS who deserve breakthrough treatments and a brighter future”

Tom, quoted by CureSHANK

Looking back, he described the nine days as “emotional, rewarding, and purposeful.” And he was clear about what they were really for.

“This was never just about running. It was about giving my son a voice, giving families like ours hope, and doing something meaningful in the face of something incredibly difficult. I couldn’t be prouder of Jenson, of our family, and of the people who supported us.”

Tom speaking to GB News
