Why Seatsy went global: from Stamford Bridge to Madison Square Garden

Tom Giles, cofounder and MD of Seatsy, on why Seatsy grew from premium hospitality into a global marketplace, with the NBA, NFL and US arenas now on the site.

Tom GilesFounder and Managing Director, Seatsy · 17 November 2026

Photo: Jean-Baptiste Bellet, CC BY 2.0

When Lewis Allsopp and I started Seatsy, the plan was always bigger than one city or one sport. We wanted to build a place where people could book the best seats at the events they care about most, wherever those events happen to be. This year we took the biggest step yet towards that, and I want to explain why it matters to the people who book with us.

From hospitality to a genuine marketplace

We went live in March 2026 with a focus on premium hospitality: the lounges, the dining, the days that people plan their year around. That is still at the heart of what we do. But the people we look after do not follow just one league or one country. Their year might include a Premier League weekend, a Grand Prix, a Knicks game and a big arena show, and we wanted Seatsy to cover all of it.

In July we answered that with a new API partnership with a US based provider. It gives Seatsy direct access to inventory for the NBA, the NFL and major US concert venues, including Madison Square Garden. At the time I said:

“This new API partnership is the single biggest step forward we've made from a platform perspective, and there's more to come.”

Tom Giles in the Seatsy newsroom, July 2026

I still feel exactly the same way. It changed what Seatsy is. We are no longer only a premium hospitality partner. We are becoming a proper global marketplace for sport and music.

Why American sport, and why now

More and more people plan travel around sport. A trip to New York is no longer just about the sights; for a lot of people it is about a night at the Garden. A long weekend in the States now often starts with the fixture list, and the hotel gets booked around it.

That is the behaviour we built for. You can now browse NBA tickets on Seatsy, see what is coming up at Madison Square Garden, and book a game such as Utah Jazz at New York Knicks just before Christmas. For NFL fans, there are fixtures like Cleveland Browns at New York Giants at MetLife Stadium in December. The same booking journey works for a Premier League afternoon in London as it does for an NFL Sunday at MetLife Stadium.

Inventory is only half the job

Access to more events is exciting, but it means nothing if the experience of booking is clunky. Alongside the new partnership, we rolled out improvements across seatsy.com to make it easier to choose the right block or package and to get hold of your tickets. That matters to me just as much as the events themselves.

My years working in sport taught me that people remember the whole day, not just the result. If the booking feels uncertain, the build up to the event feels uncertain too. So every order is covered by our ticket guarantee, and if you cannot see the event you want, our team will try to source it for you.

What has not changed

Going global does not mean losing what made us different. We still care most about the experiences that turn a ticket into a memory. Our hospitality experiences are still where a lot of my energy goes, from a table at Bagatelle at Stamford Bridge to a full Dublin weekend for Ireland v England in the Six Nations.

Seatsy is independent. We source tickets and packages through established hospitality providers and resale partners, and we are open about that. Trust is the product, and it is the one thing we will never compromise on as we grow.

What comes next

We are working on further integrations across the GCC, and there will be more to share in due course. The ambition Lewis and I set out on day one has not moved: one trusted place for the best seats in sport and music, wherever in the world they are.

If you are planning a trip around a game or a gig next year, take a look at what is live on Seatsy, or read more about who we are. And if there is an event you want that you cannot find yet, tell us. We want to hear about it.
